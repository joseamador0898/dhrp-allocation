\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hierarchical Risk Parity and variants.}
HRP \citep{LopezdePrado2016} replaces inverse-covariance-based
optimization with single-linkage agglomerative clustering and recursive
bisection, generating diversified portfolios that remain stable across
rebalancing periods. Subsequent work explored alternative linkage criteria
\citep{Raffinot2018}, transaction-cost-sensitive rebalancing
\citep{Roncalli2013}, and multi-step formulations.

All retain HRP's non-differentiable clustering core.

\paragraph{Differentiable optimization layers.}
\citet{Amos2017} introduced OptNet for end-to-end QP layers.
\citet{Agrawal2019} generalized via CVXPY layers, computing gradients
through KKT conditions of arbitrary convex programs.
\citet{Bolte2021} extended the theory to nonsmooth implicit
differentiation. In portfolio optimization, BPQP \citep{Pan2024BPQP}
embeds quadratic programs in neural networks for end-to-end Sharpe
training, achieving $0.65{\pm}0.25 \to 1.28{\pm}0.43$ Sharpe lifts.
\citet{Lin2024MSparse} solve $m$-sparse Sharpe with theoretical guarantees.
DHRP differs: instead of solving a generic convex problem at each
forward pass, DHRP imposes a hierarchical inductive bias via soft-gating,
which empirically wins in low-correlation regimes (commodities, crypto)
and matches the deeper theory connecting soft routing to optimal
transport \citep{Cuturi2013Sinkhorn}.

\paragraph{Soft decision trees and hierarchical learning.}
\citet{FrosstHinton2017} popularized soft decision trees for
interpretability. \citet{Popov2020NODE} (NODE) demonstrated that
gradient-trained ensembles of soft trees match XGBoost on tabular data.
\citet{Hazimeh2020TreeEnsemble} formalized differentiable tree ensembles
with conditional computation. \citet{Mao2025DiffTree} provide a
recent NeurIPS spotlight on differentiable decision trees via ReLU+Argmin.
DHRP adapts these architectures to portfolio allocation, with leaf
budgets entering an inverse-volatility blend.

\paragraph{Decision-focused learning.}
\citet{Wilder2019} introduced decision-focused learning for combinatorial
optimization. \citet{Elmachtoub2022SPO} formalized the smart
``predict-then-optimize'' framework. Recent finance applications include
\citet{ParraDiaz2025} for risk budgeting and
\citet{KimLee2025DFL} for global minimum-variance portfolios. DHRP is
decision-focused by construction: gradients of the realized portfolio
return flow through the soft-gating tree to upstream feature extractors.

\paragraph{LLMs in finance.}
Domain-specific language models such as FinBERT \citep{Huang2023} produce
embeddings used in sentiment-aware trading
\citep{Kirtac2025,Wu2023BloombergGPT}. However, recent benchmark papers
have systematically documented that LLMs do not reliably translate
text-task competence into financial decision quality:
StockBench \citep{Chen2025StockBench} shows most LLM agents fail to beat
buy-and-hold; FinBen \citep{Xie2024FinBen} reports LLM weakness in
forecasting and risk management; \citet{Tan2024AreLMs} (NeurIPS 2024
Spotlight) show removing the LLM component improves time-series
forecasting performance. Our LLM-DHRP ablation
(Section~\ref{sec:llmablation}) extends this body of evidence to the
hierarchical portfolio setting.

\paragraph{Multi-seed and Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio reporting.}
\citet{Bailey2012PSR} introduced PSR for sample-size- and
moment-corrected Sharpe significance.
\citet{Bailey2014DSR} extended this to deflate for backtest selection
bias. Recent ML finance papers report mean$\pm$std over 10--100 seeds
\citep{ParraDiaz2025,Pan2024BPQP}, and the BetterBench meta-analysis
\citep{Reuel2024BetterBench} explicitly critiques benchmarks lacking
multi-seed error bars.
DHRP-8 adopts these standards.
